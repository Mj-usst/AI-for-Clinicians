# Answers · Check your reasoning and score your work

Use this page after completing the exercises, for self-checking or instructor scoring. The [source page](sources.en.md) provides the complete paper metadata, key facts and original locations. This page explains the corrections and what a satisfactory submission contains. A filled table does not mean every item was checked in the original; verification status must reflect actual reading.

## Correct the six errors · 2 points each, 12 total

### E1 · Give the number its correct unit

P1 enrolled **50 physicians**, not 50 cases. Physician counts, case counts and scored responses are different units; one physician may complete several case tasks.

**Possible correction:** “P1's randomized trial enrolled 50 physicians and used six written case vignettes.”

**Locate the evidence:** [P1's setting and units, with Methods/Results locations](sources.en.md#p1).

### E2 · Identify the simulated setting

P3 used simulated text consultations. Scenarios must not be relabelled as real outpatient encounters.

**Possible correction:** “P3 assessed the system and physicians in simulated text consultations.”

**Locate the evidence:** [P3's setting and original Methods locations](sources.en.md#p3).

### E3 · Preserve the actual comparison

P3 compared the model alone with physicians. It did not allocate physicians to AI-assisted and unassisted groups to test a collaborative gain.

**Possible correction:** “P3 compares the system's and physicians' performance in simulated dialogue; it cannot directly estimate the effect of physicians using AI assistance.”

**Locate the evidence:** [P3's comparison and original study-design locations](sources.en.md#p3).

### E4 · A nonsignificant difference does not prove equivalence

P1 reported an adjusted between-group difference of **2 percentage points, 95% CI −4 to 8 percentage points; P = .60**.

**Possible correction:** “This trial did not find a statistically significant difference in the primary score between AI assistance and conventional resources. The result does not establish exact equivalence.”

The interval includes effects in both directions. A nonsignificant result by itself establishes neither “absolutely no effect” nor equivalence, and it does not demonstrate superiority.

**Locate the evidence:** [P1's primary outcome and original Results locations](sources.en.md#p1).

### E5 · Keep percentage points separate from relative percentages

P1's adjusted difference was **2 percentage points**, not a relative improvement of 2%. Also avoid presenting the unadjusted difference between group medians as if it were the adjusted effect estimate.

**Possible correction:** “The adjusted between-group difference was 2 percentage points; report its confidence interval alongside it.”

As arithmetic only, moving from 74% to 76% is an increase of 2 percentage points and a relative increase of about 2.7%. This arithmetic example does not re-estimate the trial's effect.

**Locate the evidence:** [P1's main findings and effect units](sources.en.md#p1).

### E6 · Task scores do not establish patient benefit

These studies differ in setting, comparisons and outcomes. Performance on cases or simulated tasks cannot establish fewer diagnostic errors or better patient outcomes in real clinical care.

**Possible correction:** “The selected papers provide performance evidence for specific case-based or simulated tasks. Collaborative effectiveness, safety and patient outcomes in real clinical care still require direct study.”

**Locate the evidence:** check the setting, outcomes and limitations of [P1](sources.en.md#p1), [P2](sources.en.md#p2) and [P3](sources.en.md#p3). Model-alone performance from one paper cannot replace the conclusion across the three.

### Award each correction's two points consistently

- **1 point: facts and inference.** Identify the error and rewrite it accurately. Award 0 for retaining that item's incorrect conclusion or writing only “wrong” without correcting it.
- **1 point: traceability.** Give the paper ID, a specific original section/table or course-card field, and an honest verification status. Award 0 for “the paper says so” without a location, or claiming to have read material that was not read. A course-card-only check can earn this point when its location and status are stated honestly.

## Check the P2 guided exercise

Use [P2's card](sources.en.md#p2) to check participants, sample units, study conditions and outcome locations. A satisfactory row separates physician counts from written case counts, identifies the assistance and comparator accurately, and limits its conclusion to the task performance actually measured. Keep the collaborative condition, conventional search condition and model-alone performance distinct. This guided exercise adds no points to the 20-point total.

## Check the P3 independent assignment · 8 points

### What the evidence row should contain

Check title, year and DOI against [P3's card](sources.en.md#p3). The setting is simulated text consultations. Record **159 scenarios**, **20 primary care physicians** and **20 patient actors** as separate units; do not label them as real patients. The comparison is the research system AMIE alone versus physicians, not physicians with versus without AI assistance.

The main finding is higher differential diagnosis accuracy for AMIE in expert assessment. Use the published version's numbers and locations supplied on the card. Limitations should include the simulated setting, the specific research system and the absence of a direct test of gains from physicians using AI. Source locations and verification status depend on your own reading.

### Sample conclusion · 171 English words

The count below uses whitespace-separated words, including hyphenated terms as single words. This is a **150–200 English-word** assignment. The Chinese version uses a separate Chinese-character requirement, not an equivalent length unit.

> Tu and colleagues compared AMIE alone with primary care physicians in simulated text consultations, using 159 scenarios with patient actors. Expert assessment found higher differential diagnosis accuracy for AMIE in this setting. This supports a limited judgment about the research system's performance on the tasks studied, rather than about outcomes for real patients. For our question about clinicians using a language model versus conventional resources, the study provides indirect or background evidence: it did not compare clinicians with AI assistance against clinicians using conventional resources. We therefore cannot estimate a collaborative benefit from this comparison. Nor can higher performance in simulated dialogue establish fewer diagnostic errors, improved patient outcomes, or the safety of routine clinical use. To assess those claims, further studies would need to test clinicians using the relevant system within an appropriate clinical workflow, include a clearly defined comparator, and measure safety and patient outcomes. The model, setting, assessment methods, and source locations should remain visible when this evidence is shared, with any unread information marked as awaiting verification.

**Basis:** [P3's design, comparison, outcomes and limitations](sources.en.md#p3). This is one satisfactory response; exact wording is not required. “More research is needed” should specify the missing comparison or outcomes.

### Score four areas · 2 points each

1. **Metadata and traceability — 2 points.** Award 1 for paper ID, title, year and DOI matching the published version. Award 1 for traceable locations covering setting/sample units, comparison and main results, with an honest verification status.
2. **Study identification — 2 points.** Award 1 for correct setting and sample units. Award 1 for an accurate model-alone-versus-physician comparison, outcomes and main finding, without invented numbers.
3. **Relevance to the question — 2 points.** Award 1 for identifying P3 as indirect/background evidence for our collaboration question. Award 1 for explaining that it did not directly test gains from clinicians using AI versus conventional resources.
4. **Conclusion boundaries and expression — 2 points.** Award 1 for avoiding claims of established real clinical benefit and specifying the need for the relevant comparison and clinical outcomes; a slogan without an evidence gap earns 0 for this part. Award 1 for a coherent conclusion of **150–200 English words** consistent with the table.

Use **Not reported in the original** for a genuinely unreported field, and **To be checked** when you have not read it. Honest gaps are preferable to invented information, but incomplete work is still scored on what was actually submitted.

## Apply the suggested pass rule

**20 points = 12 for the six corrections + 8 for the P3 assignment.** Both conditions must be met:

- A total of at least **16/20**.
- **E2, E3 and E6 each receive the full 2 points** for an accurate correction and source location. The independent conclusion must also avoid those errors: treating simulation as real care, treating model-alone comparison as clinicians plus AI, or claiming established patient benefit.

If the submission does not pass, revisit the relevant setting, comparison and outcomes before revising. This is a suggested classroom rule, **not a validated scale, a clinical competence assessment or certification to use AI**.
